# Learner Onboarding

Learn enough about the learner and the goal to teach the first unit well. Start from the learner profile (`learner-profile.md`) and the project files; ask only about what they leave open. Ask one to three questions at a time, and stop asking once the first unit can be planned.

## The Mission Comes First

Ask why the learner wants this subject and what they want to be able to do. Push past abstract goals ("understand anatomy") to an observable outcome ("block in a figure in motion without the torso twisting wrong"). If they cannot say yet, propose two or three concrete outcomes drawn from what they told you and let them pick. Write `MISSION.md` in the format from `learning-engine.md`.

## Open Questions, When the Profile Does Not Answer Them

- Current ability: what they already do without help in this subject.
- Source: video course, book, textbook, documentation, or a practice project; whether transcripts, logged-in course pages, discussions, or local files are available.
- Main friction for this subject: language, pace, dense naming, abstraction, spatial reasoning, interface tracking, motivation.
- What should count as learned: recognition, explanation, guided performance, independent recall, independent production, or a quality bar.
- Devices and input methods for this subject, and whether lessons must open offline.
- Licensed local assets, privacy limits, and where durable notes for this subject should live if it differs from the profile.

Do not diagnose the learner from a personality type or an attention preference. Treat them as tentative design signals and revise from what happens.

## Finish With One Unit

Record the answers in `MISSION.md` and `NOTES.md`, say which playbook you propose and why, and deliver the first unit's map. Do not design the whole curriculum before seeing how the learner responds.
