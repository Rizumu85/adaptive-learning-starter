# Learning From a Book

## First Session

Read the project instructions and inspect the contents plus one candidate section. Separate the learner's long-term goal from today's scope. If the starting point is unknown, propose one short unit and ask whether the learner has already read it; do not assume chapter one or transfer progress from another subject.

Deliver the unit as a reading edition of its pages (see "Reading Editions" below); a book not in the learner's language gets a bilingual edition from the first unit. In conversation, say which pages the unit covers and where to stop. Do not write a separate reading-map file: viewing maps belong to video and software courses. Stop after handing over this unit. Do not process later chapters to keep working while the learner reads, unless the learner has authorized the whole book ("Whole-Book Production" below). A requested whole-book outline may cover headings, but is not authorization to summarize every page or build every lesson.

## Source Locations

For scans, inspect actual page images before attributing claims to the book. Keep PDF page numbers and printed page labels distinct. Record verified pairs; never generalize one offset across the book without evidence. When locations conflict, flag them and verify the next unit before issuing exact reading instructions.

Keep source claims, paraphrases, and supplemental explanations distinguishable. Do not convert an illustrative claim into a numerical simulation unless the model is justified and its limits are clear. Passing implementation tests does not validate the science or interpretation.

## Reading Loop

1. Hand over the reading edition for the selected short unit and name the stopping point in conversation. A whole chapter is not the default unit.
2. Let the learner read and report what they noticed or where they became confused.
3. Reconstruct the missing reasoning with concrete observations appropriate to this subject, then name the concept. Do not reuse a fixed analogy across topics.
4. When useful, suggest one small application suited to the learner's goal. For drawing, this could be identifying a visible landmark or making a quick structural sketch; do not prescribe the same exercise for every section.
5. Choose what is worth retaining with the learner. Start with the lightest sufficient medium; build an interactive artifact only for a specific obstacle or an explicit request.
6. Record the learner's confirmed reading position, attempt, unresolved question, and next action. Source inspected by the agent, artifact completed, learner read, and learner demonstrated understanding are different states.

## Dense Sections

When a section lists more names, variations, or steps than the learner's goal needs, apply "Filtering Dense Material" in `learning-engine.md` before the learner tries to memorize it. A learner saying a section is "too detailed" or "I can't tell what matters" is the signal. Keep the book's text complete; change the emphasis and the check, not the source.

## Whole-Book Production

A learner may authorize the whole book at once ("这本可以把全文整理了"). That is a production task, not a change to the learning loop: build every unit's reading edition in stages, then go back to teaching one unit at a time from the recorded checkpoint. Producing a unit still proves nothing about reading or learning.

- Work in stages across all units rather than unit by unit: page ranges and offsets → OCR draft and proofreading → figure candidates, matching and crop record → translation batches → review → second-edition supplements → build, layout check, backup, commit. Inside the layout-check stage, finish one unit completely before the next (`layout-review.md`); the other stages run across all units. Each stage has one checkable output per unit, so a later session can see at a glance where the line stopped.
- Keep a per-unit state table in `NOTES.md` with the stage names above as columns (source pages inspected, proofread, machine draft, reviewed, crops cut, built). Update it whenever a stage finishes for any unit, and before starting a long background job.
- Long jobs (translation queues, cropping, OCR of a second edition) run in the background; write their command, log path and expected end state into the checkpoint before launching them. A session can be cut off at any time, and the next one must be able to tell a finished job from a stalled one without rerunning it.
- Translation batches that end with one or two leftover items waste a model call; fold a small tail batch into the previous one.
- Group the per-unit machine-translation batches into a few larger files for the runner, and let the runner skip batches whose output already exists, so a rerun after an interruption costs nothing.

## Reading Editions

A book project's units are delivered as reading editions by default: each unit's pages as a web page, with the original beside a translation when the book is not in the learner's language. The learner profile or the project's `AGENTS.md` may turn this off. The edition is the source, not the teaching.

- Keep the original text and the book's structure. Do not insert AI explanations, reading questions, or editorial notes into the edition. Orientation and teaching stay in conversation.
- "The whole book" means its body: the chapters, plus a preface or introduction when it says something about the content. Leave out what the reader already replaces: the book's own table of contents, index, half-title and title pages, copyright and catalogue-data page, blank pages, series lists, and publisher advertisements. Do not rebuild a printed contents page or index inside a chapter; the reader's contents page and its links do that job. Acknowledgments, an author biography, picture credits, a bibliography or an afterword may be kept when they carry something worth reading, but only at the end, after the last chapter, on one quiet page ("关于本书" or the book's own heading) listed last and set apart in the contents, never between chapters. Record what was left out in the project's notes so a later session does not "complete" it.
- The reading column is centered in the window at every width. When a page mixes widths (a wide figure, a two-column comparison, a side note), the narrow text column still sits in the middle; a column pushed to the left with an empty band on the right is a layout bug, usually a container sized for a wider layout that is not in use (a parallel view that is switched off, a side column that is absent). At 1440 and 1920 pixels wide, the space left and right of the body text should differ by no more than about 24px. Figure size, including the height limit, is defined in step 5 of `media-workflow.md`.
- Verify every page against the scan before calling it checked. OCR and text layers are drafts. Keep printed page labels and PDF indices distinct, and record verified pairs rather than a global offset.
- Draft text from scans and image-only books with PP-OCRv5 through RapidOCR: `assets/ocr/ocr_draft.py` runs it locally and offline on PDF pages or page images and writes each page's lines with boxes on the 1000-wide scale of `crops.json`. One recognizer covers simplified and traditional Chinese, Japanese and English. On verified pages from three books it missed or misread 3 of 2440 characters on simplified scans (PP-OCRv4: 20), 4 of 972 on traditional Chinese and English captions (PP-OCRv4: 17), and 55 of 2632 on Japanese (Windows OCR: 335), at about five seconds a page. Use a text layer instead when the PDF has a reliable one. For English set in informal or hand-lettered fonts (comic lettering, Comic Sans-like faces) PP-OCRv5 drops word spaces and whole lines; run a second engine (on Windows, the system OCR through PowerShell `Windows.Media.Ocr`; it is not shipped with this skill, and on other systems any second recognizer serves), merge the two line by line and mark every line whose letters disagree, so proofreading can concentrate on the marked lines (about a third of them in one 180-page book). The output is still a draft: check every line against the page, and look by eye at reading order across columns, ruby, text over drawings, light text on dark ground and handwriting. Record the engine and version in the project's notes.
- Crop figures from the source as described in `media-workflow.md`, keep them beside the passage they illustrate, and make them inspectable with `image-preview.md`.
- A reading edition is made for the person reading it: every choice of crop, order and layout is judged by whether it makes the page easier to read and understand, not by whether a procedure was followed.
- Read each page's layout before cutting it up, and carry the relations it draws into the web page: which pictures belong together, which text each belongs to, what each note points at. A designed spread cut into separate small figures stacked in reading order leaves pictures nobody can interpret. The rules (typeset compositions are rebuilt in HTML; hand-annotated clusters stay one picture; markers stand in for leader lines; caption widths; checks at three screen widths) and the procedure with its tool are in `layout-review.md`. Review every unit that way before a book's first delivery.
- Drawings on paper (pencil, ink, sketches, diagrams on a white or tinted page) lose their paper by default, so the drawing sits on the reader's paper instead of in a box. The method, and the few cases that stay opaque with the reason written on the crop entry, are in step 2 of `media-workflow.md`. Check this on the first unit's figures before handing them over.
- When the original and the available translation are different editions (a first edition in the original language, a later edition's translation), decide the base text first (normally the edition the learner reads in the original) and record page offsets per edition separately, verified on several pages each. Figures shared by both editions can be cut from the clearer scan with the crop naming its source `edition` and that edition's `page` (see "Crop Record" in `media-workflow.md`); when the place in the other edition matters too, record it in a neutral field such as `other_pages: {edition: page}`; match them automatically (SIFT or similar features) and confirm every match on a contact sheet before cutting. Whole sections only the other edition has go in a clearly labelled supplement block at the end of the unit they belong to, quoting that translation with its page; sections that merely swapped illustrations are not collected. Keep the translation as a reference for terms and wording, not as the text.
- A glossary, bibliography or reading list needs its own item types in the source data rather than being forced into paragraphs: a `term` entry keeps the headword and the definition apart (the headword's translation is reviewed by the agent, the definition can go through machine translation), and a `list` entry is reproduced in the original language without translation and shown in every view. Record which such items were kept and which front or back matter was left out.
- Matching every heading, quote attribution and term translation is the agent's job, not the machine translator's; the build should refuse to run while any of them is missing rather than fall back to the original.
- With two translations, compare them passage by passage and choose the clearer wording without blending them into a text neither translator wrote. Keep the changes and reasons in a local editorial file.
- For a machine translation, send short batches, keep the raw output and the reviewed version separately, and review against the source for omissions, invented content, terms, numbering, and image correspondence. Write the instruction block once per book and include, besides the term list: do not add emphasis (asterisks, italics) that the source does not have; do not add parenthesised glosses or the original word after a term; keep book and film titles in the original unless a standard translation exists; name the subject's terms that models confuse (in a figure-drawing book: hips versus buttocks, mass as 体块). Reasoning models add exactly these decorations, and removing them is most of the review when the instructions do not forbid them. Preserve the author's voice: first person stays first person, hedges and feelings stay, and splitting a paragraph into points is paragraphing, not summarizing.
- For Japanese with furigana, bind each reading to its word with semantic `ruby`, choose readings from context, and leave uncertain proper-noun readings unannotated rather than guessing.
- Keep the source's own sidebars (tips, boxed notes) as distinct asides with a light background, including continuations on later pages; keep main prose outside them.
- A reading aid for rare characters is optional: when the learner reads in a language whose text includes rare or commonly misread characters (for example uncommon anatomical characters in Chinese), annotate them with `ruby` at their first appearance in each chapter only, leave the text itself unchanged, and skip added practice blocks. Ask before adding it to a new book.
- Regenerate pages from data and validate that every source paragraph and translation appears exactly once and in order; a navigation or styling change must not change the text.
- When the text keeps naming things the learner cannot picture (plants, species, minerals, historical figures) and the book itself has a catalogue of them elsewhere, connect the two: append to each chapter a short section built from the book's own catalogue entries for the names that chapter mentions (its photo and opening description, translated), link every mention to it, and let the reference card show it in place. Mark the few names worth learning (those in the chapter's own pictures) with a short study hint, set apart from the source by its tinted box and side rule rather than by a "not part of the book" label, and the `rc-key` marker; the rest stay lookup-only.
- When the learner asks for a topic the book has no chapter for (for example plants for a waterside scene), and asks for it as notes, build a personal-notes page in the same reader: gather what the book itself says about the topic from wherever it is scattered (catalogue entries, combination lists, captions), quote each piece exactly with its printed page, translate it, and arrange it for the learner's use. Mark it by its eyebrow ("个人笔记"), place it after the book's chapters in the contents and the chapter order, and keep the book's wording and the added arrangement apart. Check lists against the printed pages: an EPUB edition can drop items.
- When the book has no picture of something the learner needs to see, and the learner asks for outside pictures, use real photographs with open licences (Wikimedia Commons: CC BY, CC BY-SA, CC0 or public domain), never AI images. Query metadata first (licence, JPEG, size, no AI-generated or illustration categories), choose by looking at the thumbnails (the whole plant or object clearly shown, identification consistent with the book's description), list file, author and licence for the learner, and download only after they agree. Credit author and licence with links in the caption; the book's own pictures always come first.
- Use the shared navigation in `reader-chrome.md` for multi-page readers. Link references in the text to their figures and passages, and add multi-target references where one phrase needs a passage and a figure (or several figures) from different places; `reader-chrome.md` ("References") lists when to add them.
- Record every figure cut from the book in `local-reading/crops.json` (see "Crop Record" in `media-workflow.md`) and run the crop audit before publishing a chapter.
- Keep source text, translations, crops, and editorial data in ignored local directories. Host them only as `private-delivery.md` allows.

Reading-edition typography is a learner choice; see `presets.md` for the bundled Chinese reading pairing.

## Existing Projects

Keep existing materials. If their accuracy or authorization is unclear, label them as drafts or historical records rather than silently deleting them or counting them as learned. Do not overwrite a claimed learning history with invented certainty in either direction. Resume from the learner's last confirmed checkpoint.
