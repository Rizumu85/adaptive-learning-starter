# Learner Onboarding

Build a lightweight learner profile before prescribing a study system. Ask one to three questions at a time.

Discover:

- The subject, practical goal, and reason it matters.
- Current experience and what the learner can already do without help.
- Source format: video course, book, class, documentation, or practice project.
- Main friction: language, accent, pace, working-memory load, abstraction, spatial reasoning, motivation, or tool operation.
- Desired note home and available tools.
- Preferred review style: concise notes, diagrams, practice checklists, retrieval questions, or demonstrations.
- Whether browser login, transcripts, course comments, or local files are available.

Do not diagnose the learner from a personality type or attention preference. Treat these only as tentative design signals and revise from observed feedback.

Create or update:

- `MISSION.md`: goal, real-world outcome, and current learning boundary.
- `NOTES.md`: stable teaching preferences, known knowledge, rejected repetitions, tool constraints, and media choices.

End onboarding with one small lesson or viewing map. Do not design the entire curriculum before observing how the learner responds.
