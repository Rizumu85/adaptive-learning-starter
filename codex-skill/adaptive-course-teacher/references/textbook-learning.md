# Textbook and Exercise-Led Learning

## Establish the Source

Identify the edition, lesson, and printed page numbers. Record PDF page indices separately when they differ. Inspect the supplied text and relevant images before preparing an exercise. Keep original quotations distinct from added teaching material; state when a source page is unavailable rather than inventing it.

Choose one observable outcome from the learner's current work. Age, textbook grade, recognition, or fluent typing do not establish independent production ability.

## Read, Demonstrate, Practice, Correct

1. Give a short reading map: what to notice and what the learner will do afterward. Use pages or exercise identifiers instead of fabricated video timestamps.
2. Let the learner read or listen to the selected passage. Check one central relationship or meaning before adding explanation.
3. Demonstrate a prerequisite that the learner has not acquired, such as forming a character, constructing a sentence, or setting up a calculation.
4. Offer guided practice where useful, then remove answer-bearing support for independent production. Apply the recall checks in `interactive-courseware.md`.
5. Correct the specific observed error. Distinguish remembering the content, carrying out the method, and the quality of the result.
6. Choose a short transfer task: use the word in another sentence, explain a new example, or solve a related problem. Do not mistake copying for transfer.

Use the learner's requested input method. Typed and handwritten responses may support different goals; keep them separately saved and assessed when relevant. Make handedness, guide lines, font choice, practice quantity, and narration optional project preferences rather than universal defaults.

## Evidence and the Next Lesson

Record the source, task, assistance used, actual response or submission link, observed error, feedback, and next action. Mark self-assessment as self-assessment. A completed page, saved draft, or generated lesson is not evidence of mastery.

Set review from observed accuracy and effort. Initial intervals are a starting hypothesis, not a universal memory curve; no fixed number of repetitions proves learning. Where outcomes have not been observed, record them as unknown and keep scheduling provisional.

Start the following lesson with any relevant unresolved prerequisite, then continue from the checkpoint. Keep future lessons adjustable rather than producing a whole textbook before testing the method.
